[Full paper (PDF)](The_Little_Girl_Who_Gave_the_World_Fire.pdf) · [Original publication](https://zenodo.org/records/24223543) · [Persistent DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24223543)

## Suggested headline

What survives when AI keeps the knowledge and loses the teacher?

## One-sentence summary

Dean Ammons examines how a machine can retain a learned capability while the evidence identifying its contributor disappears, and what that means for provenance, attribution, and contribution.

## Short press description

A girl teaches a robot to make fire. Generations later, machines still know how, but the record of who taught them has vanished. *The Little Girl Who Gave the World Fire* uses this thought experiment to introduce a mathematical framework for AI influence and provenance. Dean Ammons follows information through feedback, storage, training, and subsequent model generations, separating the persistence of knowledge from the evidence needed to identify its origin. The paper combines formal constructions with a controlled small-model experiment and considers how contributions to a defined outcome can be measured.

## Why this paper matters

- **Creators and attribution:** Keeping a useful capability does not necessarily keep the evidence identifying its source. The paper asks which records would make a specific attribution claim supportable.
- **Training and feedback:** Source-dependent evaluations can carry information into later learning even when the numerical learner does not receive the original document.
- **Value and compensation:** A measured contribution to a chosen outcome and a decision about payment require distinct models and governing rules.

## What the paper reports

The reported experiment adapts 256 output coordinates of a pretrained transformer with approximately 14 million parameters. Across eight synthetic source assignments, mean next-token accuracy on held-out wordings of trained keys is **96.09% with source-derived feedback** and **57.81% with matched independent replacement**.

These figures concern the restricted adaptation described in the manuscript. The paper also examines teacher-score transfer to fresh students and shows how external provenance can differ while numerical inputs and learned weights remain identical. See the abstract and Section 18, especially Sections 18.4, 18.6, and 18.8.

The mathematical treatment separates causal influence, recoverability, historical attribution, and economic allocation. It identifies observation boundaries under which origin cannot be uniquely reconstructed. Legal entitlement remains a separate question.

## Interview questions

1. What is the difference between demonstrating that a work influenced a model and identifying the history of how it arrived there?
2. How can a feedback score transmit information without reproducing the original work?
3. What records would need to survive across model generations to support a later attribution claim?
4. Why does measuring a contribution leave questions of rights and payment unresolved?
