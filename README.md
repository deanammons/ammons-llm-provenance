# The Little Girl Who Gave the World Fire: Information Influence and Provenance Framework

**Dean Ammons**

*Training, Attribution, and the Preservation of Origin*

**Information Flow Series · Paper III · November 14, 2026**

**A learned capability can survive the disappearance of the evidence identifying its contributor.**

[Read the paper (PDF)](The_Little_Girl_Who_Gave_the_World_Fire.pdf) · [Original publication on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/24223543) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24223543) · [Press kit](PRESS_KIT.md)

## The question

A little girl teaches a robot to make fire. The knowledge spreads through generations of machines while the record of her contribution disappears. Eventually, a machine charges her to learn what she originally taught it.

The thought experiment asks a precise question: what can survive of a contribution when the capability remains but the evidence of its origin does not?

## Overview

This paper develops an Information Influence and Provenance Framework for following information through context, evaluations, storage, optimization, and later model generations. It distinguishes source admission, causal influence, retained information, recoverable disclosure, historical attribution, and economic allocation.

The framework connects the behavioral influence questions of Paper I with the tracer and recovery questions of Paper II. It then asks what a later observer can establish about a work's contribution, what provenance must preserve, and how contributions to a specified outcome can be allocated.

## What the paper develops

- **A shared information lifecycle.** A mathematical account of how source-dependent information can move through responses, feedback, storage, training, and later generations.
- **Four mathematical systems.** Response provenance; feedback admission and containment; generational inheritance tracing; and counterfactual contribution with separately governed payments.
- **Limits on recovering origin.** Constructions in which a capability persists while different histories become indistinguishable to an observer with specified access.
- **A controlled small-model experiment.** A restricted adaptation of a pretrained transformer examines source-derived feedback, matched controls, teacher-to-student transfer, and the separation between numerical learning and external provenance.

## Research scope

The paper combines mathematical results under stated assumptions, exact constructions, and a reported controlled experiment. Its empirical findings apply to the specified model, tasks, and adaptation procedure. Historical attribution depends on available evidence and observer access; numerical contribution and legal entitlement require separate premises.
